From-To list

A run of lowercase x is the rack number, and its length sets the zero padding: AZxxx gives AZ001, AZ002… Other text stays as typed, as in DH1-R-xx. Braces list prefixes: {AZ,BC}xxx gives each prefix the same number of racks, filling every AZ rack first, then the BC racks. A range covers every prefix between its ends: {AA-AZ}xxx is AA, AB… AZ, and ranges mix with plain prefixes, as in {AA-AD,BC}xxx. Each rack holds one model, picked at random, and fills from RU 1 up in pairs with one empty RU after each pair, never above RU 36: 24 servers for a 1U model, 14 for a 2U model. Only each prefix’s last rack is partly full. The R640 and DL360 take 1U, and the R740 and DL380 take 2U. Each device lands in the same RU of the matching destination rack.

Racks are named NAP.Sector.Pod.Row.Rack, as in 14.01.09B.02.03. A sector holds 40 pods: pods 01–10 in each of the pod rows A–D, written 09B for pod 09 in row B. Pods fill a sector in order (01A–10A, 01B–10B, through 10D), then the next sector. A pod is 20 racks of 51 RU, racks 01–10 in rows 01 and 02. Rack 02-05 is the network rack: 1U switches at RU 51, 49, 48, 46, 45, 43 and 42, and 2U switches at 39–40, 37–38, 34–35 and 32–33. Every other rack has 1U switches at RU 49, 50 and 51 and 25 servers of one 1U model (R640 or DL360): one at every other RU from 1 to 29, then 10 stacked directly at RU 31–40. Each device keeps its pod, row, rack and RU at the destination; only the NAP and sector change.
